# Smart Link rebrand shortlist

Date: 2026-05-31

## What the product currently says it is

Repo and landing-page copy are pretty consistent:
- "One AI-ready link for local businesses to get discovered, contacted, and booked"
- Built first for barbershops, salons, and local services
- Core loop: profile page + contact buttons + booking + reviews + QR sharing + structured data for search/AI
- The strongest wedge is not "link in bio" vanity. It is local-business conversion: one shareable business page that works across maps, QR, chat, and search.

That means the next name should feel:
- local/business-facing, not creator/influencer-facing
- broad enough to grow beyond salons
- specific enough to avoid generic sludge like Smart Link, Link Hub, Biz Page, etc.
- plausible for domain ownership without a seven-figure knife fight

## Best naming directions

### 1) Local presence / real-world storefront
Best fit. The product is tied to physical businesses, maps, QR, walk-ins, hours, and booking.

Good signals:
- main street
- place
- front door
- local visibility
- presence that converts

### 2) Distribution / discovery signal
Also strong. The product helps a business radiate across QR, messaging, maps, and AI/search.

Good signals:
- beacon
- spoke
- path
- signal
- reach

### 3) Booking-first names
Useful, but narrower. Good if you want immediate clarity; bad if you want room to grow into broader business presence and AI discovery.

### 4) Avoid
- anything with "smart" in it: dated and generic
- anything with raw "link" in the brand: screams tool, not business product
- anything that sounds like SEO snake oil or a dentist SaaS from 2018
- names too salon-specific if you want local-services expansion

## Candidate shortlist by direction

### A. Local presence / storefront

1. Mainlio
- Rationale: has a subtle Main Street feel without being trapped in one vertical; sounds modern and ownable.
- Domain note: exact `mainlio.com` looked likely available via quick RDAP check; first move should be exact `.com`, then common defensive variants.

2. Placekind
- Rationale: warm and local-business-friendly; feels like a product that helps small businesses present themselves well.
- Domain note: exact `placekind.com` looked likely available; check trademark overlap next because the word shape is gentle and brandable.

3. Walkvale
- Rationale: suggests neighborhood traffic and footfall, which matches salons, barbers, and service businesses surprisingly well.
- Domain note: exact `walkvale.com` looked likely available; worth checking because it is more metaphorical than descriptive.

4. Shophalo
- Rationale: suggests a digital presence surrounding the shop; decent fit for reviews + booking + discovery.
- Domain note: exact `shophalo.com` appears registered; if the name survives top-five review, test owner outreach or fallback to `.app` / modified `.com`.

5. Frontiva
- Rationale: polished and software-ish without sounding enterprise-dead; hints at storefront/front desk/front door.
- Domain note: exact `frontiva.com` appears registered; good naming shape, weaker ownership odds.

### B. Discovery / distribution signal

6. Nearspoke
- Rationale: strong match for one central page distributed across nearby/local channels; "spoke" quietly implies hub-and-spoke distribution.
- Domain note: exact `nearspoke.com` looked likely available; this is one of the better ownership bets.

7. Serviceory
- Rationale: blends service + directory/story; fits AI-readable presence, services, reviews, and booking.
- Domain note: exact `serviceory.com` looked likely available; definitely worth deeper screening.

8. Bizbeacon
- Rationale: very clear on discovery/visibility, but a bit B2B-generic and less elegant than the top options.
- Domain note: exact `bizbeacon.com` appears registered; not my first fight.

9. Linkstead
- Rationale: steadiness and home-base vibes are good, but the raw "link" element still drags it toward utility-tool territory.
- Domain note: exact `linkstead.com` appears registered; also structurally weaker as a premium brand.

10. Countertap
- Rationale: has real-world service energy and taps/transactions, but it is more tactile than strategic.
- Domain note: exact `countertap.com` appears registered; more interesting as a campaign/product feature than company name.

### C. Booking / conversion-led

11. Booklane
- Rationale: crisp, clear, easy to explain. Good if you want the brand to scream appointments first.
- Domain note: exact `booklane.com` appears registered; clarity is high, ownership odds are not.

12. Booksmith
- Rationale: polished and memorable, but it pushes the brand toward booking software rather than broader business presence.
- Domain note: exact `booksmith.com` appears registered; also likely crowded from other booking/productivity uses.

13. Openchair
- Rationale: instantly good for salons/barbers, but too niche if you want to expand into other local-service categories.
- Domain note: exact `openchair.com` appears registered; vertical specificity is both the strength and the problem.

14. Deskvale
- Rationale: evokes front desk operations and local service, but sounds more internal/ops than customer-facing.
- Domain note: exact `deskvale.com` looked likely available; decent backup-tier candidate.

15. Streetbook
- Rationale: memorable and urban, but slightly overloaded because it mixes location with booking in a way that can read like a city guide.
- Domain note: exact `streetbook.com` appears registered; likely too crowded anyway.

## Ranked top 5 for deeper screening

### 1. Nearspoke
Why it wins:
- Best match to the actual product: one business page pushed across nearby, physical-world channels
- Feels ownable instead of generic
- Broad enough for salons now and other local services later
- Exact `.com` looked likely available in a quick RDAP pass

Risk:
- Slightly abstract; needs good tagline/copy support on day one

### 2. Serviceory
Why it wins:
- Strong fit for service businesses
- Holds booking, reviews, listings, AI-readable presence, and business profile in one word
- Feels like a real software brand, not a feature label
- Exact `.com` looked likely available

Risk:
- Slightly invented; some people will ask how to spell it once

### 3. Mainlio
Why it wins:
- Has local/Main Street energy without sounding old-fashioned
- Short, soft, and brandable
- Doesn’t trap you in booking-only positioning
- Exact `.com` looked likely available

Risk:
- Meaning is less obvious out of context; brand story has to do a bit of work

### 4. Placekind
Why it wins:
- Warm, human, small-business-friendly
- Easy to stretch into a broader brand world around local presence and trust
- Exact `.com` looked likely available

Risk:
- Slightly soft; less sharp/tech-forward than Nearspoke or Serviceory

### 5. Booklane
Why it stays in the top group despite ownership pain
- Crystal clear for early customers
- Strong product-story alignment if the wedge remains appointment booking first
- Better than generic junk like Smart Link because it actually points to the core job

Risk:
- Exact `.com` appears registered
- Too booking-first if the bigger vision is broader local-business presence

## My blunt take

If you want the safest practical direction, push deeper on:
1. Nearspoke
2. Serviceory
3. Mainlio

Those three feel the most founder-usable: distinct enough to own, broad enough to grow, and still tied to what the product actually does.

If you want maximum immediate clarity instead of long-term brand room, Booklane is the obvious move — but it is also the most likely to box you in and drag you into domain/trademark sludge.

## Next screening pass I would run

For the top 3-5 names:
1. exact `.com` WHOIS/RDAP re-check from multiple sources
2. quick Google search for live products/companies using the same or near-identical name
3. USPTO/EUIPO first-pass trademark screen in software/local business services classes
4. spoken test: say it aloud to 5 people and ask them to spell it back
5. homepage test: put each name into the current hero line and see whether it sounds like a real company or fake startup perfume

## Names/directions worth avoiding

- Smart/AI-prefixed names (`SmartLink`, `AISalonLink`, etc.)
- Generic utility names (`LinkDesk`, `BizProfile`, `LocalPage`, `ServiceLink`)
- Trendy misspellings that look cheap (`Lynk`, `Qwik`, `Lokl`)
- Salon-only names unless you want to stay salon-only forever
