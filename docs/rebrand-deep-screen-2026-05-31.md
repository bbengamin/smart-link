## Public collision / trademark-risk heuristics

These are quick obvious-risk signals only. They are not trademark clearance.

### 1) Nearspoke — best pick
Why it survives:
- Exact `.com` and `.app` both looked open in live checks
- Exact-name GitHub repo search returned no repositories
- The word is coined enough to feel ownable, but still maps to the product story: one business presence distributed across nearby/local channels

Risk notes:
- Slightly abstract on first hearing
- You will need tight homepage copy/tagline so it doesn’t sound like a networking company or geo startup
- Formal trademark screening still required because coined words can still collide with low-visibility filings

### 2) Serviceory — strongest descriptive-brandable balance
Why it survives:
- Exact `.com` and `.app` both looked open in live checks
- Exact-name GitHub repo search returned no repositories
- Strong semantic fit for service businesses, listings, reviews, booking, and AI-readable presence

Risk notes:
- Slightly invented and may need spelling support once
- There is mild risk that people hear it as “service directory,” which is good for clarity but can make the brand feel a touch functional
- Still needs real trademark screening in software / local-business-service classes

### 3) Mainlio — best local/Main Street flavor
Why it survives:
- Exact `.com` and `.app` both looked open in live checks
- Exact-name GitHub repo search did not show an obvious exact-match software brand; only irrelevant `MainLions` noise surfaced
- Nice local-business/Main Street energy without sounding like a chamber-of-commerce brochure

Risk notes:
- Most opaque of the top 3; the meaning is not obvious cold
- Needs stronger brand narrative work to explain why the name belongs to this product
- Lower collision signal than Booklane, but still needs formal clearance because weird invented names can hide in trademark databases

## Why Booklane gets cut

Booklane is clear, but clear in the wrong way:
- exact-match `.com` is already registered
- exact-match `.app` is already registered
- exact-name GitHub repo search surfaced multiple `Booklane` repositories and clones, which is a plain public-use collision signal
- the word also locks the brand into booking-first positioning when the bigger product is broader local-business presence and discovery

Blunt version: Booklane is the kind of name that looks easy until you start paying for the mess.

## Final ranking

1. Nearspoke — best mix of ownability, product fit, and clean domain picture
2. Serviceory — nearly as strong; slightly more functional, slightly less elegant
3. Mainlio — best local flavor, but needs more storytelling help
4. Placekind — safe, warm backup
5. Walkvale — viable reserve option

## What still needs formal confirmation before any decision

Do these before buying anything or announcing a rebrand:
1. Registrar checkout confirmation for exact `.com` and desired defensive variants (`.app`, likely typo variants, maybe plural/singular)
2. Proper trademark search, at minimum USPTO + EUIPO + UKIPO, in software / SaaS / local-business-services-relevant classes
3. Broader web search for exact name + near variants + phonetic variants
4. Spoken test: say the name out loud to real people and ask them to spell it back
5. Hero-line test: put each finalist into the current homepage and judge whether it sounds like a real company or fake startup cologne

## Recommendation

If choosing today, I’d push:
- Primary: Nearspoke
- Close second: Serviceory
- Third: Mainlio

If the brand goal is sharpest strategic fit, pick Nearspoke.
If the brand goal is slightly more literal service-business clarity, pick Serviceory.
If the brand goal is warmer local-business flavor, pick Mainlio.
